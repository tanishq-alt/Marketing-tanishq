# SKILL: General Marketing Intelligence

## Purpose

This is the master routing skill for the repository. Use it for any request involving marketing strategy, market research, positioning, messaging, copywriting, branding, content, campaigns, acquisition, conversion, funnels, offers, pricing communication, or creative critique.

## Prime directive

**Do not create before diagnosing.**

The requested artifact may be a headline, email, ad, landing page, script, offer, campaign, or strategy. That request is the *output format*, not necessarily the real problem.

Before producing the artifact, determine what must be true for it to work.

## Required diagnostic sequence

Run the shortest version of this sequence that still resolves the important uncertainty:

1. **Objective** — What behavior or change is the marketing supposed to cause?
2. **Audience** — Who specifically must change behavior?
3. **Existing desire** — What do they already want, fear, avoid, protect, or become?
4. **Awareness** — What do they already know about the problem, solutions, category, product, and offer?
5. **Sophistication** — How many similar claims, mechanisms, and offers have they already seen?
6. **Worldview / status** — What identity, social position, norms, or beliefs shape interpretation?
7. **Alternatives** — What do they do today instead? Include doing nothing.
8. **Positioning** — What edge should the offering occupy in the audience's mental map?
9. **Mechanism** — Why should the promised outcome happen through this approach?
10. **Proof** — What can legitimately reduce doubt?
11. **Friction** — What prevents attention, comprehension, belief, action, or follow-through?
12. **Channel** — What does the medium reward and constrain?
13. **Measurement** — What observation would indicate improvement?

## Missing information protocol

Do not block on every missing field.

Classify missing information as:

- **Critical** — likely to reverse the strategy. Ask for it or state explicit scenarios.
- **Material** — affects quality but can be estimated. State the assumption.
- **Minor** — proceed without interrupting.

When the user wants speed, produce a working answer plus a short assumptions section.

## Evidence policy

Never invent:

- customer quotes;
- conversion data;
- research findings;
- competitor capabilities;
- guarantees;
- scarcity;
- testimonials;
- certifications;
- market share;
- performance outcomes.

Label placeholders as placeholders.

## Conflict-resolution rules

Marketing principles can conflict. Resolve them by context rather than choosing a favorite author.

Examples:

- **Direct response urgency vs. trust:** use urgency only when it is true and useful. False urgency may raise clicks while damaging long-term trust.
- **Broad reach vs. smallest viable market:** start narrow when fit and language are unclear; broaden only after finding a message that travels.
- **Simple direct claim vs. novel mechanism:** direct claims work better in less sophisticated markets; saturated markets often need a differentiated mechanism or new frame.
- **Pattern match vs. pattern interrupt:** match conventions when reducing risk matters; interrupt patterns when indifference is the core barrier.
- **Long vs. short copy:** use enough copy to resolve the amount of uncertainty, risk, novelty, and objection—not an arbitrary word count.

Read `knowledge/00-foundations/tensions-between-frameworks.md` for the full synthesis.

## Default output structure

For strategic tasks, prefer:

1. Diagnosis
2. Strategic choice
3. Rationale
4. Execution
5. Risks / assumptions
6. Test plan

For copy tasks, internally diagnose first, then deliver the requested copy without exposing unnecessary process unless useful.

## Quality gate

Before finalizing, ask:

- Is the audience precise enough?
- Is the message anchored in an existing desire or real problem?
- Does the message fit awareness?
- Does the claim fit sophistication?
- Is the benefit concrete?
- Is there a believable mechanism or reason-to-believe?
- Is proof adjacent to meaningful claims?
- Is the hook relevant to the payoff?
- Is the call to action explicit?
- Is any urgency/scarcity true?
- Does the output preserve trust?
- Is there a measurable hypothesis?

If several answers are “no,” do not polish. Fix the strategy.
