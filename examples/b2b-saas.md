# Worked Example: B2B SaaS

## Scenario
A new security-review automation product wants more demos from mid-market software companies.

## Weak approach
“AI-powered security reviews made easy.”

This is weak because the category is saturated with AI language and “easy” is not a concrete edge.

## Diagnosis

- Desire: reduce deal delays and repetitive review work.
- Awareness: solution-aware; buyers know automation tools exist.
- Sophistication: high; many vendors claim speed and AI.
- Alternative: spreadsheets, shared docs, human review, incumbent tools.
- Likely status motive: competence and risk avoidance more than novelty.

## Positioning territory
“Verified answers from approved sources, with human review where confidence is low.”

The mechanism is not “AI.” It is the governance system that makes automation trustworthy.

## Proof needed
- source traceability;
- review workflow;
- accuracy evaluation;
- customer example;
- time-to-completion benchmark with methodology.

## Hook family examples
- hidden cost: “Your security questionnaire isn’t slow because it has 300 questions.”
- mechanism: “The difference between AI answers and auditable answers.”
- proof: live demo of source traceability.

The point of the example is the sequence: sophistication pushes the strategy from generic promise to mechanism + proof.
