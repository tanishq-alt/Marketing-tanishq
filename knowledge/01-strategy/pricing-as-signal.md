# Pricing as a Signal

Price is not only arithmetic. It is also information.

Buyers may interpret price as a signal of:

- quality;
- risk;
- prestige;
- seriousness;
- category position;
- expected service level;
- scarcity;
- buyer identity.

## Pricing communication questions

- What comparison does the buyer naturally make?
- Is the purchase evaluated as cost, investment, insurance, convenience, or status?
- Does a lower price reduce perceived risk or create suspicion?
- What hidden costs of the alternative should be surfaced?
- Is the pricing unit aligned with how value is experienced?

## Reframing price ethically

You can improve price perception by changing perspective, not facts:

- cost per use;
- cost per user;
- cost relative to alternative;
- cost relative to avoided waste;
- total cost of ownership;
- time saved;
- risk transferred.

Never disguise material fees or use deceptive anchoring.
