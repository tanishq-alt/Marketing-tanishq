# Positioning

Positioning is the deliberate choice of what the offering should mean relative to alternatives in the mind of a specific audience.

## Positioning is not a slogan

A slogan expresses positioning. It does not create it.

A positioning choice should answer:

- For whom?
- In what situation?
- Against which alternative?
- On which decision dimensions?
- With what trade-offs?
- Why should the audience believe the difference is real?

## The edge map

Choose two dimensions the audience genuinely cares about. Plot alternatives. Look for an edge worth owning.

Good dimensions are decision-relevant:
- speed;
- control;
- convenience;
- customization;
- prestige;
- transparency;
- simplicity;
- depth;
- safety;
- price certainty;
- flexibility;
- service intensity.

Avoid dimensions every competitor claims, such as “quality” or “innovation,” unless you can operationalize them.

## Strong positioning behavior

Strong positioning makes some prospects say “not for me.” If every buyer can interpret the claim as ideal, the position is probably vague.
