# Differentiation and Mechanism

## Mechanism

A mechanism explains *how* or *why* the promised outcome occurs. It turns a bare claim into a causal story.

Mechanisms matter most when:

- the audience has heard the core promise repeatedly;
- competitors make similar claims;
- skepticism is high;
- the process itself is meaningfully different;
- technical proof can increase belief.

## Mechanism hierarchy

1. **Feature mechanism** — a concrete capability enables the outcome.
2. **Process mechanism** — a different workflow produces the result.
3. **System mechanism** — multiple components reinforce one another.
4. **Business-model mechanism** — incentives/economics enable a different customer experience.
5. **Evidence mechanism** — measurement, verification, or visibility makes the outcome more reliable.

## A credible mechanism has

- a clear cause-and-effect chain;
- meaningful difference from common alternatives;
- enough specificity to inspect;
- proof proportional to the claim;
- no magical leaps.

## Mechanism brief

> Because we do **X** differently, **Y** happens, which reduces/increases **Z**, producing the promised outcome.

Then challenge each causal arrow.

## Failure modes

- renaming a standard feature and pretending it is proprietary;
- jargon as mechanism;
- “AI-powered” with no explanation of what AI changes;
- mechanisms that sound impressive but do not matter to the buyer;
- claiming causality that evidence does not support.
