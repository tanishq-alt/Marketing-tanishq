# Hook Point System

A hook point is a compact attention device that creates enough relevance, curiosity, tension, novelty, or value for the audience to continue.

## What a hook can be

- phrase;
- visual;
- opening action;
- statistic;
- question;
- demonstration;
- contrast;
- format;
- personality;
- unusual combination of familiar elements.

## Three-second design

For fast-feed content, the opening should communicate at least one of:

- who this is for;
- what problem is being addressed;
- what payoff is coming;
- why this is different;
- what tension needs resolution.

Do not interpret “three seconds” as a license for nonsense. The first moment sets an expectation that the rest of the content must satisfy.

## Hook creation workflow

1. Identify audience desire/problem.
2. Identify the content's actual payoff.
3. Generate 5–7 hook families, not 20 wording tweaks.
4. Create 3 variants per family.
5. Remove hooks that cannot be paid off quickly.
6. Test openings separately from full concepts when possible.
7. Track downstream quality, not only view-through rate.

## Hook fatigue

A hook loses effectiveness when the audience sees the same pattern repeatedly. Refresh by changing:

- point of view;
- proof;
- mechanism;
- story format;
- visual language;
- audience segment;
- timing/context;
- degree of specificity.

Do not refresh by making the same claim louder.
