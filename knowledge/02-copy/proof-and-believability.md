# Proof and Believability

Persuasion is constrained by what the audience can believe.

## Proof ladder

From generally weaker to stronger, depending on context:

1. unsupported assertion;
2. explanation / mechanism;
3. demonstration;
4. specific example;
5. customer quote;
6. multiple independent customer examples;
7. quantified internal data with methodology;
8. third-party verification;
9. controlled comparison / experiment;
10. repeated real-world performance.

The right proof depends on the claim.

## Architecture of belief

Do not drop the largest claim before the reader has the premises needed to accept it.

Build a sequence:

`accepted fact → implication → mechanism → evidence → stronger claim → proof → action`

This is more persuasive than stacking adjectives.

## Claim-proof adjacency

Put proof near the claim it supports. Distance creates cognitive work and skepticism.

## Proof inventory

Collect:

- demos;
- screenshots;
- process evidence;
- product specs;
- benchmarks;
- before/after data;
- case studies;
- testimonials;
- reviews;
- certifications;
- expert validation;
- guarantees;
- usage data;
- transparent limitations.

## Verification rule

The bigger the claim, the stronger and more inspectable the proof must be.
