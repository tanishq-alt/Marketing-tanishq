# Long vs. Short Copy

There is no universally correct copy length.

## Copy should get longer when

- the product is expensive;
- the product is unfamiliar;
- the category is risky;
- switching costs are high;
- there are multiple decision makers;
- the market is skeptical;
- the mechanism is novel;
- there are many objections;
- the asset must complete the sale without a salesperson.

## Copy can be shorter when

- intent is already high;
- the audience knows the product;
- the decision is low-risk;
- the offer is simple;
- the CTA is only a small next step;
- prior brand trust carries the argument.

## Rule

Use the shortest copy that resolves enough uncertainty for the requested commitment.

Short copy that omits necessary proof is not concise; it is incomplete.
Long copy that repeats the same idea is not persuasive; it is undisciplined.
