# Headlines and Hooks

A headline or hook has one immediate job: earn the next unit of attention from the right audience.

## Hook quality dimensions

Score each 1–5:

- **Relevant** — clearly connected to a meaningful audience concern.
- **Useful** — promises value, insight, relief, or progress.
- **Specific** — concrete enough to feel real.
- **Distinctive** — not indistinguishable from category clichés.
- **Believable** — does not trigger more skepticism than curiosity.
- **Open loop** — creates a reason to continue without withholding the entire value.
- **Fit** — matches the channel and audience awareness.

## 4U-inspired stress test

Ask:

- Is there a legitimate reason to care now?
- Is there a fresh angle?
- Is it concrete?
- Does it serve self-interest?

Do not force all four. A hook can be excellent without urgency.

## Hook families

Use these as strategic families, not fill-in-the-blank formulas:

- direct benefit;
- problem recognition;
- mechanism reveal;
- proof / demonstration;
- counterintuitive insight;
- specific question;
- comparison;
- before/after transformation;
- identity / “for people who…”;
- consequence / hidden cost;
- teardown / diagnosis;
- case study;
- challenge to a limiting assumption;
- news / novelty;
- useful information promise.

## Hook-body contract

The body must cash the check written by the hook.

If the hook promises a diagnosis, diagnose.
If it promises an outcome, explain how and under what conditions.
If it implies evidence, provide evidence.

Clickbait is not “a strong hook.” It is a broken hook-body contract.
