# Audience Awareness Levels

Awareness determines how much context the message must provide and what the opening can assume.

## Five-level model

### 1. Unaware
The audience does not recognize the problem/desire in the form relevant to the offer.

**Message job:** earn identification and curiosity before selling.

Use:
- worldview;
- situation;
- symptom;
- hidden cost;
- story;
- identity;
- pattern interruption tied to a real tension.

Avoid leading with product name, price, or detailed features.

### 2. Problem-aware
The audience recognizes the pain or goal but not the category of solutions.

**Message job:** clarify the problem and make a solution path plausible.

Use:
- problem articulation;
- consequences;
- desired future;
- category education;
- “why this keeps happening” mechanism.

### 3. Solution-aware
The audience knows solution categories but not necessarily your product.

**Message job:** differentiate the approach.

Use:
- mechanism;
- comparative frame;
- trade-offs;
- reason-to-believe;
- proof.

### 4. Product-aware
The audience knows your product but is unconvinced or unmotivated.

**Message job:** resolve doubt and increase preference.

Use:
- proof;
- use cases;
- objection handling;
- superiority on relevant dimensions;
- offer structure;
- risk reversal when legitimate.

### 5. Most-aware
The audience knows the product, value, and fit; the remaining issue is timing, friction, or offer.

**Message job:** make action easy.

Use:
- clear offer;
- availability;
- price / terms;
- concrete CTA;
- genuine urgency.

## Mixed-awareness audiences

Most channels contain mixed awareness. Solve this by:

- segmenting distribution;
- using layered pages;
- writing an opening broad enough to be understood but specific enough to select the right audience;
- routing traffic to different pages by intent.

## Audit question

For every major section of a message, ask:

> What must the reader already believe for this sentence to work?

If the answer assumes knowledge they likely lack, the message is skipping steps.
