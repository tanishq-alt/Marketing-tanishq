# Marketing as Change

## Definition

Marketing is best understood as the deliberate design of a change in perception, preference, behavior, identity, or social adoption—not merely promotion.

This framing prevents a common mistake: treating distribution as the beginning of marketing. Distribution matters only after there is a coherent answer to **who should change, from what, to what, and why**.

## The four changes

A marketing program can seek one or more of these changes:

1. **Perception** — “I see this problem/category/brand differently.”
2. **Preference** — “Among alternatives, I now favor this one.”
3. **Behavior** — “I click, try, buy, refer, renew, switch, or act.”
4. **Identity / norm** — “People like me do this; this choice says something about me.”

## Change brief

Before producing tactics, write:

- Current state: what the audience thinks/does now.
- Desired state: what must be different after marketing works.
- Barrier: why the current state persists.
- Trigger: what could make reconsideration rational or emotionally compelling.
- Proof: what makes the new state believable.
- Action: what observable behavior confirms movement.

## Diagnostic questions

- Are we trying to create awareness, shift preference, or convert existing intent?
- Is the main barrier ignorance, indifference, skepticism, habit, risk, status, cost, or friction?
- Does the product itself deliver the promised change?
- Is the desired behavior proportional to the amount of trust we have earned?

## Failure modes

- Measuring impressions when the actual goal is preference.
- Asking cold audiences for a high-commitment action without enough trust.
- Trying to “get the word out” before the offering has a sharp audience and promise.
- Mistaking a creative refresh for a strategy.
