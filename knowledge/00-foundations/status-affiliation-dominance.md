# Status, Affiliation, and Dominance

Many purchase decisions are partly social. Functional utility explains what a product does; status dynamics often explain why one acceptable option wins over another.

## Two status orientations

### Dominance
Vertical comparison: ahead/behind, winner/loser, premium/basic, expert/novice, leader/follower.

Signals include:
- exclusivity;
- rank;
- scarcity;
- superiority;
- elite endorsement;
- performance leadership.

### Affiliation
Horizontal comparison: insider/outsider, belonging, shared identity, “people like us.”

Signals include:
- community;
- social proof from peers;
- shared rituals;
- references;
- collaboration;
- norms and language.

## Application

Ask:

- Does the audience seek to rise in rank or belong to the right group?
- What choice would increase or threaten their perceived status?
- What story will they tell others after choosing?
- Who must approve socially, professionally, or internally?

## Guardrail

Do not assume your preferred status orientation matches the customer's. A founder may sell dominance while the customer wants safety and affiliation.
