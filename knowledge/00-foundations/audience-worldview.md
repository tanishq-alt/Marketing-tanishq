# Audience Worldview

## Principle

People interpret the same message through different beliefs, experiences, identities, norms, and stories. Demographics can describe a group; worldview helps explain how that group interprets meaning.

## Worldview map

Document:

- What do they believe is true about the problem?
- What do they distrust?
- What do they blame?
- What do they admire?
- What feels “for people like me”?
- What language feels native versus corporate?
- What trade-offs are acceptable?
- What would make them feel foolish, unsafe, or out of place?
- What would make them feel smart, responsible, modern, independent, caring, powerful, or connected?

## Psychographic segmentation

Prefer segments defined by meaningful differences in decision criteria, not decorative personas.

Weak segmentation:
- “Millennial Maya, age 34.”

Useful segmentation:
- “Wants maximum control and will trade convenience for auditability.”
- “Wants the category to disappear into the background and will pay for simplicity.”

## Message implication

A message should not try to win an argument against a deeply held worldview unless the product requires that change. More often, connect the offer to a story the audience is already willing to believe.
