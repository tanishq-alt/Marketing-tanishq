# Trust, Attention, and Permission

## The sequence

Attention lets the message enter.
Relevance gives attention a reason to continue.
Trust allows the audience to accept claims and take risk.
Permission makes future attention cheaper and more reliable.

Treat them as different assets.

## Attention

Attention can be borrowed through novelty, relevance, urgency, conflict, specificity, visual interruption, or social context. Attention that is disconnected from the eventual payoff is usually low-quality attention.

## Trust

Trust grows through:

- kept promises;
- accurate claims;
- transparent trade-offs;
- demonstrated competence;
- consistent behavior;
- relevant proof;
- customer experience;
- repeated useful interactions.

Trust falls when marketing over-promises what delivery cannot support.

## Permission

Permission is not merely legal ability to contact someone. Operationally, it means the recipient expects and values the message enough not to treat it as unwanted interruption.

Permission assets include:

- opted-in email audiences;
- subscribers;
- communities;
- recurring customers;
- followers with demonstrated engagement;
- users who actively request updates.

## Strategic implication

Rented attention can produce demand now. Permission and trust lower the cost of future demand.

Do not optimize one campaign so aggressively that it destroys the next ten.
